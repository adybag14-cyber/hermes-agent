Hermes Agent Fork

Privacy policy

Effective date: 9 September 2026 · Android package: com.mobilefork.hermesagent

This policy applies to Hermes Agent Fork, the independent community Android app published by the maintainer of adybag14-cyber/hermes-agent. It is not official Nous Research or Teknium software.

Your chats are not automatically uploaded to the publisher, and the app does not send publisher analytics. Local model inference runs on your device. Requests to a remote AI provider go to the service you select. The publisher operates a separate private service that receives only AI-content reports you explicitly review and submit. Local inference does not make separate downloads, browsing, speech services or network-enabled tools offline.

Which edition are you using?

The full GitHub/F-Droid edition includes optional device tools, Linux/package workflows and background automations. The separate Play edition being introduced with version 0.13.156 is a foreground AI client: it excludes Linux installation, autonomous device control, broad installed-app access, accessibility and notification-listener services, privileged Shizuku actions and persistent background agents. Its local inference engine is packaged with the app; model downloads are data, not executable-code updates. Read the edition label and controls in your installed app. Features described below apply only where that edition provides them.

1. Information used on your device

The app uses and may store chat messages and responses, conversation history, selected attachments, downloaded models, workspace files, provider configuration and credentials, preferences, and task or automation records on your device. Device tools can read information needed to perform the actions you enable. This information may include screen content, notifications, calendar entries, location, installed-app information and device diagnostics.

What the app can access depends on the feature, your Android permissions, any selected files or folders, and enabled integrations. Do not place passwords, financial information or other sensitive information in prompts or accessible files unless you intend it to be used by the selected feature.

2. User-selected remote providers and services

If you configure a remote AI provider, the app sends the submitted prompt and the conversation context needed for the request to that provider. Selected attachments, images, audio or transcriptions, and relevant tool results may also be transmitted when the feature uses them. For example, configuring an OpenAI API key and using that provider sends the request content to OpenAI. Your configured credentials authenticate requests to the selected service.

Version 0.13.156 introduces a separate in-app remote-processing disclosure and affirmative consent for the selected provider and configured endpoint. Declining does not send the pending message; an on-device model remains an alternative. Changing the provider or endpoint requires a new consent. You can revoke remote-processing consent in Settings → Privacy and safety. Revocation stops new app requests and requests cancellation of active work, but cannot recall data already received by a provider.

Network-enabled tools, provider portals, embedded web pages, browser actions, external integrations and model downloads connect to their respective services. Those services can receive request content and network information such as your IP address. Speech-recognition services may process voice input according to your device and provider settings. Other apps receive information when you use an action that shares information with them.

The publisher does not receive these app requests through a publisher-operated collection backend. Each third-party service has its own privacy policy, security practices and retention rules. Review the service and endpoint you configure before use. Choosing local inference does not prevent a separately enabled tool or integration from connecting to the internet.

3. Android permissions and optional device features

  • Microphone and camera: voice input and camera-based input when you use these features.
  • Files and folders: attachments, model import, workspace operations and folders you grant through Android's file picker.
  • Accessibility access: inspecting visible screen content and performing supported UI actions when you enable the service. Screen text or screenshots used as remote-model context may be sent to your selected provider.
  • Notification access: reading notifications for enabled device workflows. Notifications can contain private messages and other sensitive content. Relevant results used in remote requests may be sent to your selected provider.
  • Calendar and location: calendar workflows and location-related tools or watchers you enable. Relevant results used in remote requests may be sent to your selected provider.
  • Installed-app visibility and usage access: identifying, launching or inspecting apps and providing device-workflow information.
  • Nearby devices, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and NFC: supported connectivity and device tools.
  • Notifications, overlays, foreground services and startup: displaying status or floating controls and running enabled runtime, watcher or automation features. Some enabled features continue while the main app screen is not visible.
  • Optional Shizuku integration: additional device operations only when configured and authorised through that integration.

You can revoke permissions and special access in Android Settings and disable integrations or automations you no longer want. Denying access can prevent the related feature from working.

In the full edition, version 0.13.156 adds a prominent accessibility disclosure before opening Android's permission screen. Android's permission switch is not treated as consent to that disclosure. The service checks app consent before accessing screen events; the app offers consent revocation. Hermes is a general-purpose assistant, not a disability accessibility tool. In the Play edition the accessibility service is absent.

The voice-input disclosure explains that your selected Android speech-recognition app may send microphone audio to its own provider. An offline preference is not a guarantee of local processing. Hermes receives the recognized text in the composer; sending that text to an AI provider is a separate action.

4. Publisher collection and support

Optional in-app AI-content reports: the Report AI content action lets you review and edit up to 4,000 characters from a selected answer, choose a reason, and optionally add up to 1,000 characters of notes. Only after you choose Submit report does the app send that text, reason, notes, app version, edition and a random report identifier to https://hermes-content-reports.adybag14.workers.dev. No other chat history or attachments are included. Free text may contain personal information you choose to include; remove unnecessary personal data and secrets first. Common credential patterns are redacted on the server, but this is not a guarantee that every secret or personal detail will be detected.

Reports are used to investigate offensive or unsafe AI output and improve safety controls. They are stored in a private Cloudflare D1 database and are not exposed through a public report feed. The app retains a private deletion receipt on your device; the service stores a hash of its secret, not the secret itself. The service uses a day-scoped keyed hash of the connecting IP address for abuse prevention, with rate-limit records expiring within two days and hourly cleanup. It does not store the raw IP address in the reports database. Cloudflare processes connection information to provide and secure the service under its privacy policy. The report database uses D1's EU jurisdiction; this does not mean every network or edge-processing operation occurs in the EU.

Reports do not create a Hermes account and do not subscribe you to notifications. They are not an emergency channel, and submission does not guarantee an individual response. The reporting service does not receive normal chats automatically or run app analytics.

If you voluntarily email support or post a repository issue, the publisher receives the information you provide, including your email address and message. Use this channel only for information needed to address the request. Repository issues are public; do not post API keys, private conversations or personal files there.

Support information is used to respond to your request and investigate the reported issue. Email and repository services process that communication under their own policies.

5. Storage, retention and deletion

Local information remains on your device until it is removed through the relevant app controls, Android app-storage controls or file-management tools. Clearing this app's storage or uninstalling it removes its app-private data under Android's normal behaviour. Exported files, files in shared folders and downloads stored outside app-private storage may remain and must be deleted separately.

Reports: active report records are scheduled to expire after 30 days, with cleanup running hourly. In versions with report controls, use Settings → Privacy and safety → Report deletion receipts to request deletion sooner. A confirmed deletion removes the active record. If delivery or deletion is uncertain, the app keeps the receipt so you can retry. Delete submitted reports before clearing app data if you want to use that receipt; clearing storage or uninstalling does not itself contact the report service. If you lose the receipt, contact the privacy address below with the report identifier if available, without sending credentials. Cloudflare's recovery history can retain deleted data for up to a further 30 days; see D1 recovery retention. Reports are not exported to a separate publisher archive.

Removing local data does not delete copies already sent to another service. Use that service's account and deletion controls or contact it directly. Disconnecting a provider prevents future use of that configuration; revoke credentials with the provider if you want to invalidate them.

Hermes does not provide its own cross-device user account. Third-party provider sign-in and API credentials belong to that provider, not to a new Hermes account. Local data erasure and provider disconnection do not delete an OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, OpenRouter or other third-party account.

For deletion of support information held by the publisher, email adybag14@gmail.com with enough detail to identify the request, without sending credentials. Support correspondence is kept as needed to resolve the request and handle follow-up, subject to applicable retention obligations. The publisher cannot delete information controlled solely by your chosen provider.

6. Security

The app uses Android's application-storage and permission boundaries. The Android app configuration disables standard app backup and restricts cleartext traffic for its Android network components except local loopback connections. These measures are not a guarantee for every user-configured endpoint, embedded runtime, script or external integration. Use trusted HTTPS endpoints, protect your device and API keys, and review the access granted to tools.

7. This website

This privacy-policy website is a static GitHub Pages site. Its pages do not include publisher analytics, tracking scripts or advertising cookies. GitHub processes hosting requests, including network and device information, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

8. Google Play tester group

The tester signup page links to the voluntary Hermes Agent Fork Testers Google Group and explains the separate Google Play opt-in step. The page has no email-submission form, account database, analytics script or embedded sign-in widget. You sign in and choose membership on Google’s own pages; this website never asks for your Google password.

Group owners and managers can see your Google Groups membership, display name and email address to administer test eligibility. The member list and member email addresses are restricted to owners/managers; posting is restricted to the owner. Google processes membership, subscription preferences and Play testing choices under its own policies. Membership notifications or owner announcements may be delivered according to your Google Groups subscription preferences; you may choose No email.

You may leave the group through Google Groups and separately opt out of the Play test through Google Play. Leaving the group can remove your eligibility for future closed-test releases. Group membership alone does not enrol you in Play testing or start a qualifying test period. We do not publish or copy the membership list into this website’s public repository. For questions about tester information administered by the publisher, use the privacy contact below; Google’s own records and retention are governed by its policies.

9. Changes and contact

Updates to this policy will be published on this page with a revised effective date. For privacy questions or requests, contact the Hermes Agent Fork maintainer at adybag14@gmail.com.
